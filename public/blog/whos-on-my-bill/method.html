How we calculate it

1. Your utility

We look up your ZIP in the EPA's ZIP-to-utility file and take the utility that serves most of that ZIP. If your ZIP is split between utilities, we may show the wrong one. Tell us and we'll fix it.

2. The price change

We compare your state's average residential electricity price in 2021 with the latest 12 months, using EIA data. This is total revenue divided by total kWh sold, so it includes supply, delivery, taxes and fees.

3. Your dollars

By default we use the average home's usage in your state (EIA, 2024). If you enter your bill, we work out your usage from it instead. Then:

increase per year = monthly kWh × 12 × (price now − price in 2021)

That's what the same amount of electricity costs you now compared with 2021.

What this number is not

  • It's a state average, not your exact bill. Your utility's own rate is shown alongside it.
  • It doesn't say why prices rose. Fuel costs, grid upgrades, storms, and capacity charges linked to data centers all play a part. We only link a part of your bill to data centers where public filings measure it, starting with PJM capacity auction costs.
  • It isn't adjusted for inflation.

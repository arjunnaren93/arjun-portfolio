[ 05 · The data centers ]

So is it the data centers?

US data centers used about 152 terawatt-hours of electricity in 2023. In a few states they already use a huge slice of all the power. Out of every 100 kWh used in Virginia, 26 went to a data center.

  • Used by data centers in 2023, out of every 100 kWh
  • Added by 2030 in EPRI's moderate scenario

If data centers drove prices, these states would top the list. They don't.

North Dakota, Nebraska and Iowa are among the biggest data center hosts for their size. Their home electricity prices rose 9%, 15% and 8%, well under the US average of 27%. Across all 44 states, there's no link between how much power data centers use and how fast prices rose.

[ 06 · The auction ]

The auction that changed the bill.

Every year PJM holds an auction that pays power plants to be ready for a future year. The cost lands on the bills of the 67 million people PJM serves. For a decade it cost $2.2 billion to $10.9 billion a year. Then the 2025/26 auction cost $14.7 billion.

[ 07 · The verdict ]

So what did raise the bill?

Not one thing. Here's how the evidence stacks up, biggest factor first.

The 3.64¢ rise per kWh, 2021 to 2025, split in two

2.57¢ · 71%Everything got more expensive. Consumer prices rose 18.8%. Electricity keeping pace would cost 16.23¢ today.
1.07¢ · 29%Above inflation. After inflation, home electricity rose 6.6%.

Consumer prices: CPI-U annual averages from BLS, via FRED. Gas: Henry Hub spot price, annual average, from EIA. Electricity: EIA.

Most of it

Inflation

71% of the increase matches prices rising across the whole economy. Utilities pay the same rising costs as everyone else.

BLS consumer prices

The 2022 shock

Natural gas

Gas cost 66% more in 2022, the year home electricity jumped most. By 2024 gas hit its lowest inflation-adjusted price on record, and electricity kept rising anyway.

EIA Henry Hub prices

The long climb

The grid itself

Utility spending on local distribution lines rose 160% from 2003 to 2023, to $50.9 billion a year. Those costs go into rates and stay there: one reason bills didn't fall with gas.

EIA, Nov 2024

Where they hit

Storms and wildfires

Berkeley Lab names extreme weather and wildfire costs among the key drivers of state price changes, alongside grid spending and gas prices.

Berkeley Lab, Oct 2025

In PJM, through the auction

Data centers

No link state by state (r = -0.11), and Berkeley Lab finds load growth has tended to push prices down. But in PJM the market monitor ties $29.4 billion of capacity costs to data center demand, shared across 13 states and DC.

Market monitor

[ The verdict ]

Most of the increase is inflation. Most of the rest is the grid, storms and a 2022 gas shock. Data centers raise bills where a market spreads their costs, the way PJM's capacity auction does.
